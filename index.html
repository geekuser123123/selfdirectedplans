Beyond the Standard Menu

Asset Categories You Can Learn About.

Self-directed accounts may hold a wider range of assets than conventional plans. We help you understand how each category works within the rules.

Real Estate

Residential, commercial, or land holdings owned by the account, subject to rules on personal use and related parties.

Private Companies

Interests in private businesses and LLCs, subject to ownership limits and prohibited transaction rules.

Promissory Notes

Loans made by the account to unrelated borrowers under written terms.

Precious Metals

Certain bullion that meets IRS fineness standards, held by an approved depository rather than at home.

Private Placements

Private funds and offerings, subject to investor eligibility and the custodian’s requirements.

Traditional Investments

Stocks, bonds, and funds can typically still be held alongside alternative assets.

Eligibility depends on IRS rules, your plan documents, and custodian policies. Some assets, including life insurance and most collectibles, are not permitted in IRAs. Listing a category is educational and is not a recommendation to invest.

Frequently Asked Questions

Good Questions Deserve Clear Answers.

General educational information only. Rules can change and individual circumstances differ, so confirm specifics with your account custodian and a qualified tax or legal professional.

What is a self-directed IRA?

A self-directed IRA is an individual retirement account held with a custodian or trustee that permits a broader range of investments than the stocks, bonds, and funds typically offered by conventional providers. The same general IRA rules still apply, including contribution limits, distribution rules, and prohibited transaction rules. The account holder is generally responsible for choosing and evaluating investments.

How is a self-directed 401(k) different?

A self-directed 401(k), often called a solo 401(k), is an employer-sponsored plan generally available to self-employed individuals and business owners with no eligible full-time employees other than a spouse. Unlike an IRA, it is a qualified plan with its own plan document, contribution structure, and administrative and reporting obligations. Eligibility and features depend on your circumstances and the plan’s terms.

Can I roll over an existing retirement account?

Many existing retirement accounts can be moved into a self-directed account through a direct rollover or a trustee-to-trustee transfer, but eligibility depends on the type of account, your current plan’s rules, and your employment status. Indirect rollovers are subject to timing limits and other restrictions. Because the tax treatment of a rollover depends on your situation, review it with a qualified tax professional before you move funds.

What types of assets may be eligible?

Depending on the custodian or plan, self-directed accounts may hold assets such as real estate, private company interests, promissory notes, and certain precious metals, in addition to traditional investments. Some assets are not permitted—for example, life insurance and most collectibles cannot be held in an IRA—and transactions involving yourself or other disqualified persons are restricted. Each custodian may also set its own limits on what it will hold.

How long does the setup process take?

Timing varies by provider, documentation, and circumstances. Factors include how quickly account paperwork is completed, the custodian’s or plan provider’s processing times, and—if funds are being moved—how long the current provider takes to release them. We can help you understand the steps involved so you know what to expect.

What type of assistance do you provide?

We provide education about self-directed retirement account structures, guidance on the documentation involved, and administrative coordination to help you navigate account establishment and potential rollover or funding steps. We do not provide investment, legal, or tax advice, and we are not a retirement account custodian.

Self-Directed IRA

A self-directed IRA is held by a custodian or trustee that permits alternative assets in addition to conventional investments. It can be structured as a traditional or Roth IRA, and the usual IRA contribution, distribution, and tax rules continue to apply.

What we help you understand

  • How self-directed IRAs differ from conventional IRAs
  • Categories of alternative assets that custodians may permit
  • Prohibited transaction and disqualified person rules at a general level
  • Account requirements, documentation, and the setup sequence
  • Options for funding through contributions, transfers, or rollovers

We do not select investments, hold assets, or provide tax or legal advice.

Self-Directed 401(k)

A self-directed or solo 401(k) is a qualified retirement plan generally designed for self-employed individuals and business owners without eligible full-time employees other than a spouse. It is established by the business and governed by a written plan document.

What we help you understand

  • Eligibility considerations for self-employed individuals and owners
  • How plan structure and contribution types work at a general level
  • Plan rules, ongoing administrative duties, and reporting obligations
  • Documentation and steps involved in establishing the plan
  • Moving eligible existing retirement funds into the plan

Eligibility and plan features depend on your circumstances. Confirm specifics with a qualified tax professional.
